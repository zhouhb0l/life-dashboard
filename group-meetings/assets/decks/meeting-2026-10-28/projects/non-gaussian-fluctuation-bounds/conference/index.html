Universal Bounds on Non-Gaussian Fluctuations in Coherent TransportSpeaker notes · press S

Good morning. I want to start from an inverse question. Suppose we measure the stationary current, its zero-frequency noise, and the third cumulant. Can those three numbers be reproduced by any two-terminal elastic Landauer transmission, or do they force us beyond that entire model class?

The key point is that I will not choose a transmission profile and fit it. At known affinity, the first two cumulants determine an exact interval in which the third cumulant must lie for every passive, elastic, noninteracting transmission, with arbitrary energy dependence and any finite number of channels. The condition is also sufficient: every point inside the interval has an elastic realization or is approached by a regular elastic limit.

I will first state and derive that interval. I will then follow the manuscript's Figure 2 from panel a to panel e: the interacting double-dot model, the parameter-space exit, the nonweak crossing, the exact elastic adversary, and the many-body correlation that tracks the exit. I will finish with what an experiment would actually need to measure.

V5 introductionNonzero C₃ alone is not a witness

The motivation is not simply that the third cumulant is non-Gaussian. Elastic coherent scattering already produces a nonzero third cumulant, so nonzero skewness by itself does not identify interactions or inelasticity.

What the mean and noise miss is visible in this schematic. Two counting distributions can have the same center and the same Gaussian width while redistributing a small amount of probability between the two tails. The third cumulant is odd in those deviations, so it detects that directional imbalance.

There are already two relevant classes of bounds. The current-noise relation constrains the Fano factor, and the known marginal fermionic result constrains normalized skewness. But they are separate tests. They do not require the measured noise and skewness to arise from the same transmission.

So the manuscript asks a sharper question: once the affinity, current, and noise are fixed, what is the complete allowed interval of the third cumulant? That conditional interval is the object on the next slide.

Here is the central result. We work with stationary particle counting between exactly two equal-temperature fermionic reservoirs. The affinity \(A\) is known and nonzero, and the current direction is chosen so that \(C_1\) is positive.

The first inequality is the exact Gaussian prerequisite. Once the measured \(F\) is inside that window, the second inequality gives the complete allowed interval for \(S\). This is not just a necessary witness. It is necessary and sufficient for the first three cumulants to admit an elastic two-terminal Landauer representation.

The nondegenerate lower face is attainable for \(\kappa-a<F<\kappa\). The upper face and formal endpoints are approached by regular limits. That construction is written as a bounded moment problem on the next slide.

03 · why the bound is complete

The transport problem is a bounded moment problem

Noise fixes one current-weighted moment; the third cumulant fixes the next.

\[x_n(E)=\mathcal T_n(E)\,[f_L(E)-f_R(E)]\in[0,a]\]

V5 Eqs. (4)–(9)Necessity, tightness, and constructive sufficiency

The derivation is short because the Landauer problem can be recast as a truncated Hausdorff moment problem.

For each energy and channel, define the directed transfer strength \(x\) as the transmission multiplied by the reservoir occupation imbalance. Fermi statistics and passive transmission constrain \(x\) to the compact interval from zero to \(a\). Weighting these processes by their contribution to the current defines the probability measure shown in the middle.

The measured noise fixes the first moment, \(m=\kappa-F\). The third cumulant fixes the second moment, \(r\). A positive measure on a bounded interval must satisfy two elementary conditions: its variance is nonnegative, so \(r\ge m^2\), and bounded support gives \(r\le am\). Substituting the measured moments gives exactly the two faces of the corridor.

The converse is also physical. One transfer strength realizes the nondegenerate lower face for \(0<m<a\). Two strengths fill every strict interior point. The upper face and formal endpoints are approached by regular limits that mix very weak processes with Pauli-saturated ones. This is why passing the test means compatibility through \(C_3\); it does not prove that the real device is elastic. Now we test the bound on an interacting system.

V5 Eq. (11) and Fig. 2aWeak- and nonweak-lead evidence

The manuscript tests the theorem on the minimal interacting conductor shown in Figure 2a: a spinless serial double quantum dot. We count net particle transfer across the right dot-reservoir contact.

The Hamiltonian contains detuning, coherent interdot hopping, and the interdot Coulomb repulsion \(U\). At \(U=0\), the model is quadratic. That is an important exact null: its full counting statistics admit an elastic Landauer description.

When \(U\) is nonzero, the physical logic changes. If one dot is occupied, the energy required to occupy the other dot is shifted. A tunneling event therefore changes the many-body state and the transition energies encountered by the next event. The transfer attempts are no longer independent trials through one fixed transmission.

We examine both weak and nonweak lead hybridization. The next slide shows the weak-coupling counted map, and the decisive nonweak line cut is evaluated with counted HEOM. Figure 2 then develops the evidence in order: parameter map, nonweak crossing, elastic boundary constructions, and microscopic correlation.

V5 Eqs. (9)–(12) and Fig. 2d–eExact exclusion plus mechanism support

This is the decisive comparison, and panels d and e belong together. In panel d, we do not compare the DQD with one convenient resonance or a fitted noninteracting model. The theorem supplies the exact global elastic extrema at the measured current and noise.

The lower construction makes every current-carrying process have one directed strength, which reaches the lower face. The upper construction mixes processes approaching zero strength with Pauli-saturated processes, reaching the opposite face as a regular limit. Both are conditioned on the same \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) as the interacting point.

The elastic interval is from 0.687773 to 0.887236, while counted HEOM gives 0.639385. The Landauer deficit is 0.048389. Because the proof already allowed arbitrary energy dependence and any finite number of channels, another resonance or channel cannot repair this mismatch.

Panel e then asks whether an independent many-body diagnostic follows the same trend. \(K_{\mathrm{irr}}\) vanishes for every number-conserving fermionic Gaussian state by Wick factorization. Along this nonweak cut, the positive deficit grows with \(-K_{\mathrm{irr}}\), with Pearson coefficient 0.94.

This supports the manuscript's microscopic story: Coulomb-conditioned occupation creates memory between successive transfer cycles. It remains an association within this model. The exclusion theorem itself does not identify a unique mechanism.

V5 experimental discussionEvent exposure is the primary requirement

The operational protocol is deliberately small. At one fixed operating point, acquire stationary counting windows and estimate the first three particle-current cumulants with their joint covariance. The affinity and current sign convention must be fixed, and the windows must satisfy the independence or mixing assumptions used in the covariance model.

Normalize to \(F\) and \(S\), evaluate the two exact Landauer faces, and propagate the covariance through the comparison. If the uncertainty-qualified value of \(S\) lies outside the interval, every fixed passive elastic Landauer transmission is excluded through third order. If it lies inside, the data are compatible; that does not prove that the real mechanism is elastic.

For the registered independent-window design with \(\lambda=C_1\tau_w=1\), and with the leading covariance propagated from cumulants through \(C_6\), the representative DQD point requires \(2.24\times10^4\) expected net transferred particles for a one-sided three-sigma test with ninety percent power.

The 22.4-second conversion assumes an effective resolved net rate of \(10^3\) per second. Detector bandwidth, missed events, dead time, and correlations between windows require device-specific calibration. The exposure is therefore a design target under stated assumptions, not a detector-complete acquisition time. With that distinction, the final message is simple.

Let me close with three points.

First, at known affinity the current and noise determine the complete allowed interval of the third cumulant for the entire two-terminal elastic Landauer class. The result is both necessary and sufficient.

Second, the interacting double dot provides a selective counterexample. It can pass the current-noise condition and the marginal skewness test while lying below the exact conditional lower face. Figure 2 then closes the evidence chain with a nonweak crossing, exact elastic boundary constructions, and a many-body correlation that tracks the deficit.

Third, the conclusion is a model-class statement. A violation tells us that no fixed passive elastic transmission can reproduce the first three cumulants. It does not, by itself, tell us which richer mechanism is responsible.

The practical message is the sentence on the slide: three stationary cumulants can falsify every effective elastic transmission without reconstructing one. Thank you, and I am happy to take questions.
